I was talking with a manager about one of their staff members who chose to exit a position recently. When the reason for their decision came up it was related to a sense that they had more growth opportunities at the organization they were transitioning into."
""
"I don't have an issue with someone exiting. Sometimes that's an amazing thing, especially when they're going to something specifically better for them. The issue I tend to have with exits I see is when the person isn't extremely honest with themselves about their why behind the move and it simply appears that they're chasing some unknown thing that will satisfy them. One of the most caring things a boss ever did for me was that I wasn't clear on my decision to exit and then gave me a day to go process. A helpful adage is "if you chase "enough, you'll never catch it"."
""
"The reason I bring that up, is that I see it too often. Someone flipping jobs because they think they'll get what they want on the other side only to figure out the grass was less green and more spray painted. The reality of most of these decisions is that life is akin to Kenny Roger's The Gambler and you're deciding when to play your cards. There is always some level of emotion in why you're looking outside of your current situation, and being truly honest is deeply beneficial in that scenario."
""
"In the situation I mentioned the math simply didn't make sense for that reasoning. Simply "back of the napkin" math between the total staff, number of backfills positions opened, and number of promotions means that 15% of total staff stayed where they were. It also means that there's a 71% chance of promotion if you stay longer than a year while being able to "get", "want", and "have capacity for" an elevated role."
""
"Comparing a 71% chance of promotion against the prospect of growth at a local service firm where you're just under the CEO, someone might see a unique possibility there, but generally I would think it's that they're not being  truly honest about the prospect of growth."
""
"Just like cards, it's best to take a more objective approach.
