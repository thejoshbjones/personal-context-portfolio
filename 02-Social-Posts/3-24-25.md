I had a client a while back that signed up for a contract with my business. Seemed like a great fit. I liked the organization, I liked my contact there, I had even done some work to help them on a volunteer basis in the past. In fact, we discounted the price to help it make sense for them. "
""
"Effectively we sold them a block of hours with no strategic relationship attached. (This was a mistake. I learned. Won't do it again.)"
""
"Then they signed paper and started paying us money. The next few years were, well difficult. Lot's of complaints about areas where they expected service that we did not, and could not do. Every so often the problem would hit my desk and I'd call them. "
"-We didn't give security guidance -> We could but weren't contracted to do it."
"-This product didn't work like we thought -> I discouraged them from adding it into the contract, but they insisted."
"-We really thought you would meet with us weekly -> We can, it'll eat into your block hours you won't be able to use engineering hours."
""
"There were a few other issues, mostly around that contact's ability to maintain clear communication. It ended up being a situation wherein my company was blamed by the organization for many problems in areas for which we weren't responsible."
""
"Finally the contact left the organization and a new contact took his place. I had by that point left my org, but was friends with the client org. New contact called me. I met with him and met with my old company and helped guide them through a renegotiation of the contract that seemed more beneficial to both parties."
""
"There's more to say on your ability to be ok being misunderstood as you build an organization or lead a team. That's for another day. The takeaway is that your job is to drive value for your organization by understanding the agreements with your partners and setting up the underlying incentives for the outcomes you need. If you experience buyer's remorse after signing an agreement, don't get mad at a vendor, you signed it. Do your work to get the value out of the relationship. "
