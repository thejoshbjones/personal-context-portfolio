"The thing about monopolies. They exist, and they are not illegal so long as you got there through solid innovation. The moment you begin doing anything that appears anti-competitive is when you get an issue. With this case, it could mean something as big as the breakup of Google, it could mean something as small as a requirement to give users default choice of search engine on browser, or it could be somewhere in-between such as what happened with Microsoft in the 90's and early '00s where the government became their product manager."
""
"Let's talk about game theory and market dynamics with these options. Generally, when I see the government step in what is touted as a big decision leads to literally no results beyond wasted energy. "
"e.g. Microsoft is still one of the most valuable companies in the world and then end result didn't change. They maintained through and are generally using their same bundled playbook."
"For Google this could also be true and here are some examples of how that might play out:"
"1) Breakup of Google -> Each respective company is still massive in it's own space. Shareholders get an extra value from the stock splitouts. We end up with something akin to AT&T where there's a slow rollup back or there's a greater consolidation of smaller like companies in each respective business' verticalized line of business and prices can't be competed away at the same level which is bad for consumers. (e.g. YouTube takes on streaming and either buys competitors, or because it's not subsidized by the other business units will join the rest in raising prices instead of decreasing prices.)"
"2) Gov becomes Google's Product Manager -> Innovation slows to a level for about a decade. Then the playbook repeats. Because of their already existing search monopoly, the question will be can they maintain for the next 10 years and still innovate enough with AI to stay in the game."
"3) They are required to remove the default search in chrome. -> The deal structure with Apple goes away. Apple offers a choice to consumers and sets up that they have a % take on revenue to the respective search choice on a per device basis. Most people choose Google as the default because they will. The revenue will generally remain unchanged."
""
"I've only listed one outcome for each decision of which there are multiple potential outcomes. On each of these outcomes, it seems the directionally accurate call is to still place bets on Google."
""
